Start with the business problem

An email automation agency and a CRM consultant overlap, but they usually solve different parts of the problem. One is often strongest in nurture and messaging, the other in pipeline structure, data and operational adoption.

If the business has no reliable sales process, fixing email sequences first can be premature. If the CRM already works but leads receive inconsistent follow-up, an automation specialist may be the more efficient choice.

Ask who is responsible for implementation

Give both providers the same current-state map and ask what they would change first. Their answers should reveal whether they understand the operational problem or are simply selling the part of the stack they know best.

Do not assume a strategy, audit or setup line automatically includes every practical change needed to make it live. Put dependencies and internal responsibilities into the proposal.

Agree how success will be measured

For CRM-led work, measure stage visibility, ownership and response processes. For automation-led work, measure delivery, replies, booked actions and whether leads move correctly when they act.

Decide the reporting model before delivery so both sides know which numbers are useful and which are merely diagnostic.

Protect ownership and handover

Keep appropriate business ownership of platforms, data and core accounts. Document administrator access, exports and what happens if the supplier relationship ends.

Ask for proof that matches the work

Ask for a responsibility map showing exactly which process, data, message and integration problems the supplier will own.

Where client information is confidential, a supplier can still demonstrate process quality through redacted documents, owned properties or implementation evidence without inventing outcomes.

Watch for this red flag

Red flag: A provider recommends rebuilding the entire stack before checking whether the current CRM or automation can simply be repaired.
