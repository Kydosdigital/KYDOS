Why Your Small Business Social Media Gets Views but No Enquiries

Getting views is encouraging, but it can be frustrating when posts rarely lead to calls, quotes or bookings. Here is how to find the gaps between social media attention and customer action.

Published 18 November 2026Small-business conversion guideOldham and Greater Manchester
First question: Can someone who sees your post find out what you sell, why it suits them and how to enquire without guessing? If not, start there before spending more on posts or advertising.

Views are not the same as customers

Social media can help somebody discover a local business, understand its work and decide whether to get in touch. But one popular Reel does not guarantee a booking. A video may be watched by people outside your service area, a customer may want more proof before deciding, or the enquiry may get lost after a click. The useful question is which part of that journey is failing.

1. Your offer is difficult to understand

Some business profiles look attractive but do not explain what is available. Make your main service, customer type and area obvious in your bio, pinned content and key posts. For example, “Mobile car valeting across Oldham and nearby areas, request a quote using the link” tells a customer more than “Passionate about excellence”.

Try this: Ask somebody unfamiliar with your business to look at your profile for ten seconds. Can they tell you what you do and how to buy?

2. You attract attention from the wrong people

A funny video might get thousands of views without reaching anyone who can use your service. For an Oldham-based tradesperson, relevance and the ability to serve the viewer matter more than broad exposure. Use actual work, local examples where appropriate and explanations aimed at the questions your real customers ask.

Try this: Compare the topics that earn views with the topics that lead to genuine questions, site visits or calls.

3. You are missing the proof customers need

Service businesses often depend on trust. Clear photographs of completed work, genuine customer feedback, useful process explainers and introductions to the team may answer objections better than another promotional graphic. Always get permission before using identifiable customer images, names or testimonials.

Try this: Pin one service explanation, one authentic work example and one practical way to contact your business.

4. The call to action is vague

“Follow for more” is a sensible call to action on a helpful tip, but it is not always right for a booking-focused post. Tell customers what to do next and what happens afterwards. “Request a quote”, “Check available appointments” or “See the current menu” are more specific than “DM us” without context.

Try this: Give one post one primary next step, instead of asking people to like, share, follow, visit a website and call simultaneously.

5. The path to an enquiry has friction

The social post may be doing its job while the booking page is broken, the bio link is outdated or the form takes too long. Open the journey on your own phone. Is the contact information visible? Does the button work? Can customers complete the form without unnecessary fields?

Try this: Follow a post all the way to a successful test enquiry. Track where you encounter delays or confusion.

6. Enquiries are not being followed up

A lead sitting in an unread inbox will not turn into a customer. Decide who owns responses, which messages are urgent and what information the team needs to reply. A CRM or simple automated acknowledgement can help, but it should not replace a useful human response.

Try this: Check the time between an enquiry and the first meaningful reply, then agree a response process that suits your opening hours and capacity.

7. You are measuring the wrong outcome

Reach, views and saves indicate different kinds of attention. To understand business value, also look at calls, booking clicks, qualified messages, form submissions and which enquiries become customers. Attribution will not always be perfect because somebody may see a social post and contact you later by phone.

Try this: Add a simple “How did you hear about us?” question, use tagged links where appropriate and record real enquiries alongside platform metrics.

When should you consider paid advertising?

Advertising can help you reach a defined audience more quickly, but it will not fix an unclear offer, weak booking page or unanswered enquiries. Before increasing your budget, make sure your service page, form or booking system and follow-up process are ready to support the traffic.

Frequently asked questions

Why do my social media posts get likes but no sales?

Likes may come from people who are interested in a post but do not need your service. Check audience relevance, proof, clarity of offer, booking links and follow-up before assuming you only need more reach.

Should a small business post more often to get enquiries?

Not automatically. Increase volume only if you can maintain relevance, quality and a clear contact route. Fix conversion issues first.

Can social media management help with lead generation?

Content can support discovery and trust. Paid campaigns, landing pages, contact forms and a follow-up process may be needed to create and manage a more deliberate lead-generation journey.
