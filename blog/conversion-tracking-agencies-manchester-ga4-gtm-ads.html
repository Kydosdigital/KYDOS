Start with the business problem

Conversion tracking is often bought after a business realises that clicks are visible but enquiries, bookings or purchases are not. The provider's job is to create a trustworthy chain from user action to analytics and advertising platforms.

The key distinction is between an attempted action and a successful one. Tracking a form button click as a lead can inflate performance when the form never submits.

Ask who is responsible for implementation

Ask the agency to document each conversion from user action through trigger, analytics event and advertising-platform destination. Where several platforms receive the same event, the setup should explain how duplicate counting is avoided.

Do not assume a strategy, audit or setup line automatically includes every practical change needed to make it live. Put dependencies and internal responsibilities into the proposal.

Agree how success will be measured

Validation should include real test actions and confirmation inside the receiving platform. For lead generation, the long-term goal is to connect qualified outcomes back to source rather than stop at raw form counts.

Decide the reporting model before delivery so both sides know which numbers are useful and which are merely diagnostic.

Protect ownership and handover

Keep appropriate business ownership of platforms, data and core accounts. Document administrator access, exports and what happens if the supplier relationship ends.

Ask for proof that matches the work

Ask for an event map and test evidence showing the same action appearing correctly at each stage of the tracking chain.

Where client information is confidential, a supplier can still demonstrate process quality through redacted documents, owned properties or implementation evidence without inventing outcomes.

Watch for this red flag

Red flag: The provider treats every CTA click as a completed lead without checking whether the form, call or booking actually succeeded.
