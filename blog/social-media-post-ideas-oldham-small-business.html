12 Social Media Post Ideas for Small Businesses in Oldham

From trades and salons to independent shops and cafés, these content ideas help you stay consistent without giving up every evening to social media.

Published 18 November 202612-post calendarOldham, Greater Manchester
Quick start: Choose three ideas per week and use content from your real business. A useful, consistent plan matters more than chasing every trend.

Why a simple content plan beats last-minute posting

Running a small business leaves little time for captions, filming and design. You already have stories worth sharing, but they are spread across finished jobs, customer questions, team activity and products. The following ideas work across many industries because they answer the questions customers ask before choosing a provider.

12 social media posts you can create this month

1. Show a finished job

A decorator could show a completed room, a baker a finished order or a hairdresser a client-approved result. Explain the customer problem, the service and one useful detail. Ask permission before showing anyone or their property.

2. Answer a real customer question

Take the question you heard three times this month and answer it plainly. A salon could explain treatment duration; a mechanic could explain when a particular service is needed.

3. Explain the booking process

Share how customers make contact, what information they need to provide and what happens after booking. Removing uncertainty helps someone take the next step.

4. Introduce your team

A brief introduction to the person providing the service builds confidence. Include their role, a specific skill and a question they enjoy answering.

5. Demonstrate one product or service

Record a short, clear video showing how an item works or what a service involves. Use genuine footage, not a made-up example of work you have never done.

6. Share a genuine review

Use a customer's own words with permission, and explain what the review relates to. Do not alter the meaning or promise that everyone will get the same result.

7. Feature a local connection

A project in Royton, a supplier in Chadderton or a genuine Oldham community partnership can provide useful local context. Avoid adding location names just for keywords.

8. Offer a seasonal tip

Trades can share winter-preparation reminders; restaurants can explain seasonal menus; shops can show realistic ordering deadlines. Match the advice to what customers need now.

9. Compare two options

Explain how customers can choose between two services, treatments or products. Make the trade-offs clear rather than claiming one is right for everybody.

10. Show your process

Share a glimpse of preparation, packing, setting up or quality checks. Explain why the step matters. Avoid filming confidential client information.

11. Correct a misconception

Pick one common misunderstanding about your service and explain the accurate answer. A useful explanation can build more trust than a generic promotional caption.

12. Present a clear offer

State what customers can book or buy, who it is for, the price or important terms where relevant and a straightforward enquiry route. Keep deadlines and availability accurate.

What if you do not have enough photos and videos?

Save a few useful pictures while working, write down recurring questions and record short demonstrations when they naturally occur. Build customer consent into the process. Where fresh filming is not realistic, genuine service explainers and well-designed graphics are a sensible alternative. Never invent case studies, reviews or customer results.

How to tell whether the posts are working

Track relevant profile visits, booking clicks, calls, quote requests and messages, not only likes or views. Ask new customers where they found you. If your posts get attention but nobody enquires, the issue may be your offer, profile or follow-up process rather than your posting frequency.

Frequently asked questions

How often should a small business post?

Choose a realistic schedule you can maintain. Three relevant posts a week can be a starting point, but the right frequency depends on your customers and content.

Should every post mention Oldham?

No. Mention a location when it adds genuine context, such as a project, event or service area.

Can I post the same thing across three platforms?

Reuse ideas where relevant, but adapt the format, opening and action for each platform.
