Social Media Management for Aesthetic Clinics: What Should an Agency Actually Do?

For an aesthetic clinic, social media should do more than keep Instagram active. It should help prospective patients understand the clinic, trust the practitioner and know what to do next.

Social media sits in the trust layer

Many prospective patients will check a clinic's social presence after discovering it through Google, a recommendation or an advert. The feed therefore needs to answer credibility questions quickly.

A useful clinic content mix

  • Practitioner-led education
  • Consultation process and what to expect
  • Clinic environment and team
  • Responsible FAQs
  • Non-promotional educational content
  • Patient journey content where consent and advertising rules allow
  • Clear routes to book or enquire
Do not build the calendar around prohibited or risky claims. Public promotion of prescription-only medicines is restricted, and cosmetic intervention ads must avoid irresponsible pressure or insecurity-based messaging.

What should a social media agency actually handle?

The answer depends on the package, but you should be clear about strategy, content planning, copy, design, video editing, posting, community management, reporting, paid social and lead follow-up. If an agency says it is managing growth, ask which of those stages it is actually responsible for.

What should the clinic measure?

Reach and engagement can show whether content is being consumed, but they are not the end goal. Track profile actions, website visits, consultation enquiries, qualified lead volume and the assisted role social plays in conversions.
