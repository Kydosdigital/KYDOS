Start with the business problem

Branding proposals can range from a logo-only project to a complete identity system with positioning, guidelines and rollout assets. Before comparing prices, decide which level of work the business actually needs.

A logo can be delivered quickly, but a usable identity also needs colour, typography, spacing, application rules and enough assets for the team to stay consistent.

Ask who is responsible for implementation

Ask how feedback is collected and who has final approval. Too many decision makers can create endless revisions, so define stages, deliverables and sign-off points before design begins.

Do not assume a strategy, audit or setup line automatically includes every practical change needed to make it live. Put dependencies and internal responsibilities into the proposal.

Agree how success will be measured

Brand work is not usually judged by short-term conversion alone. A practical success test is whether the business can apply the identity consistently across website, social, documents and campaigns.

Decide the reporting model before delivery so both sides know which numbers are useful and which are merely diagnostic.

Protect ownership and handover

Keep appropriate business ownership of platforms, data and core accounts. Document administrator access, exports and what happens if the supplier relationship ends.

Ask for proof that matches the work

Ask to inspect complete brand-guideline examples and real applications, not only isolated logo mock-ups.

Where client information is confidential, a supplier can still demonstrate process quality through redacted documents, owned properties or implementation evidence without inventing outcomes.

Watch for this red flag

Red flag: The proposal promises a 'full brand' but does not specify strategy, assets, revision limits, source files or ownership rights.

Frequently asked questions

How should I compare branding and visual identity providers?

Use the same brief and compare deliverables, implementation responsibility, ownership, support, exclusions and the business outcome the work should improve.

Should I choose the cheapest provider?

Not automatically. Compare missing implementation, software, support and handover costs before deciding.

What should be agreed before work starts?

Agree the objective, responsibilities, access, approvals, deliverables, timeline, measurement and extra-charge triggers.
