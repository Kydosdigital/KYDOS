Why Isn't My Business Showing on Google? 9 Checks to Try First

Find out whether the problem is website indexing, search ranking or your Google Business Profile before paying for fixes.

Published 21 November 2026Google visibility diagnosticGreater Manchester
Start here: A missing website and a missing Google Maps listing are different problems. First search for your business by its exact name, then inspect your website URL in Google Search Console, and finally check the status of your Google Business Profile if you are eligible for one. Being indexed or verified does not guarantee a high ranking.

First, decide what is missing

Try searching your exact business name and then your main service plus your town. If your name appears but the service search does not, you may have a relevance or ranking problem rather than an indexing problem. If your website does not appear even by name, investigate whether Google has indexed its pages. If you mean the map box, check the separate Business Profile route. A Google Business Profile is not a substitute for a website, and a website does not automatically create a Business Profile.

Nine checks you can carry out yourself

1. Search your exact business name and your main service separately

Record which search fails, your device and location, and whether you mean the ordinary website results or the map results. Results can differ between locations. Do not assume that a screenshot from one phone represents every search.

2. Inspect your website URL in Search Console

If you control the website, open Google Search Console and use URL Inspection on your main service page. Check whether Google reports it as indexed, which canonical it selected, and whether crawling was allowed. A site: search can be a rough discovery check, but it is not a reliable full index count.

3. Check for technical blocks before rewriting content

Look for an accidental noindex directive, a blocked crawl, a broken page, or a canonical pointing somewhere unexpected. A sitemap helps discovery but does not guarantee indexing. Ask your web developer for the exact URL and evidence rather than accepting 'SEO is being done' as an explanation.

4. Check whether your page actually answers the service query

A page headed only 'Welcome' may not clearly explain the service, area covered, prices or next step. Compare the actual question a customer searches with the useful information your page provides. Avoid repeating a town name unnaturally. A page can be indexed and still rank poorly.

5. Confirm whether your business is eligible for a Google Business Profile

Google generally requires a business to have a customer-facing location or to visit customers in person. Online-only businesses without in-person customer contact are not automatically eligible. A rented mailing address or virtual office that is not genuinely operated by the business is not an eligible storefront. A co-working office has additional signage, staffing and customer-reception conditions. Read Google's official eligibility and representation rules before creating or editing a listing.

6. Check verification, restrictions and duplicate profiles

If eligible, sign in to the account that manages the Business Profile. Look for verification requests, suspended or restricted status, pending changes and duplicates. A verified profile can still be absent for a particular service search. Do not create a second listing to bypass a problem.

7. Check your address or service-area settings

Businesses that travel to customers but do not receive customers at their address should generally hide the address and set a truthful service area. If you run a genuine staffed storefront, check the real address, hours and customer-facing details. Simply adding Manchester, Oldham and Stockport as service areas will not guarantee map positions in those places.

8. Review your main category, services and public information

Choose a specific category that describes what the business is, not a string of extra keywords. Check that the website, contact details and service description are accurate. Google's local results consider relevance, distance and prominence, so a competitor may appear above you even when your website looks better. Do not buy fake reviews or insert locations into your registered name.

9. Test whether a customer can actually contact you

Open the page on a mobile phone and test the visible phone, email, form or booking link. If visitors can find you but cannot enquire, that is a conversion problem rather than an indexing problem. Keep a note of the date, query and result before paying for changes.

Three Greater Manchester examples, clearly hypothetical

Oldham mobile electrician: Imagine an electrician who visits customers but has no staffed shop. A properly eligible service-area profile may help, with the address hidden, but creating a false Oldham storefront or extra listings for Rochdale and Tameside would be inappropriate.

Stockport salon: Imagine a salon with a staffed customer-facing premises. If the profile is verified but not appearing for 'hair salon Stockport', check the category, accurate location and competing local results before assuming verification failed.

Manchester B2B consultancy: Imagine a consultancy whose website is indexed for its name but not 'consultancy Manchester'. Its priority may be a clearer service page, evidence of expertise and a usable contact route. Whether it qualifies for a Business Profile depends on its real-world customer contact and premises, not on having a Manchester postal address.

Questions local owners often ask

Why does my competitor show on Google Maps but I do not?

Local results vary with relevance, distance and prominence. First check eligibility and verification, then your category, business information and the query's location. A competitor ranking above you does not by itself mean your profile is broken.

Does Google charge to put my website in search results?

No. Organic inclusion is free, but eligibility does not guarantee that Google will crawl, index or show a page for every query. Advertising is a separate paid product.

Can I use my virtual office address on Google Maps?

Not merely because you rent an address. Google says a virtual office where the business does not actually operate is ineligible. Co-working spaces have strict additional requirements. Use Google's current guidelines rather than a provider's sales claim.

Will adding more town names improve my local ranking?

Not automatically. A clear service area and helpful location-specific information are useful, but stuffing town names into copy or creating duplicate profiles does not solve relevance or distance.
