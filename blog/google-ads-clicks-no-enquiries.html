Google Ads Gets Clicks but No Enquiries: What Should You Check First?

Eight checks to find out why paid traffic is not turning into genuine customer enquiries, before you spend more.

Published 21 November 2026Eight practical checksGreater Manchester
Short answer: Clicks show that somebody interacted with your ad, not that they wanted to become a customer. Before raising your budget, check whether enquiries are recorded correctly, whether your search terms and locations match the customers you serve, and whether the landing page makes contacting you straightforward. Then inspect what happens after an enquiry arrives. A campaign can have inexpensive clicks and still lose money.

First, what does “no enquiries” actually mean?

Separate no recorded conversions from no real enquiries. If you received calls, emails or form submissions but Google Ads shows zero conversions, you may have a measurement problem. If you have verified there were no enquiries, investigate traffic relevance, the page and the offer. If you received enquiries but none were suitable, investigate lead quality and follow-up. These are different problems with different fixes.

Do not assume every conversion shown in an account is a sale. A button click, page view or form start can be configured as a conversion action without proving a completed customer enquiry. Google's conversion tracking guidance explains what conversion actions measure.

Eight checks before increasing your Google Ads budget

1. Check whether the form and telephone number actually work

On a phone, open the landing page, inspect the form, its error messages, the contact number and any booking link. Use an authorised test process, not fake customer records. Confirm whether a successful submission reaches the intended inbox or CRM. If the form does not work, changing keywords will not solve the immediate problem.

2. Verify the conversion action is measuring a real outcome

In Google Ads, review Goals > Conversions and identify which actions are included in the campaign's primary conversion goals. Check whether the tag fires on successful completion rather than just a click or page load. Review the conversion action's status and any consent or cross-domain limitations. If calls matter, check whether call reporting is configured and appropriate for the business. Google's website conversion setup guidance describes the available implementation approaches.

3. Read the actual search terms, not just your chosen keywords

Open the campaign's Search terms report. A phrase-match or broad-match keyword can lead to searches with a different purpose from the one you intended. Look for research-only questions, job seekers, unrelated services, bargain hunting and locations you do not serve. Add relevant negative keywords only after checking that they would not block genuine customer demand. Some search terms are withheld for privacy, so the report is not a complete list. See Google's search terms report guidance.

4. Confirm location targeting and location options

Check the campaign's target areas and location options. Google Ads can target people showing interest in a location as well as people physically present there, depending on your settings. A service business working only within a practical travel radius should inspect where clicks and leads actually originate before changing to a more restrictive setting. Do not assume a Greater Manchester location name in an ad automatically limits who can click. Review Google's location targeting guidance.

5. Match the ad promise to the landing page

Someone searching for a specific service in Stockport should arrive at a page that clearly explains that service, who it suits, what area is covered and how to request it. If the ad promises a consultation but the page offers only a generic agency homepage, visitors may leave. Make the headline, service detail, contact path and mobile layout consistent with the searcher's expectations.

6. Check whether the offer is suitable for the searcher

A click may be relevant but still not lead to an enquiry because the service, availability, price expectations or trust signals do not match. Where appropriate, explain what is included, typical process, location coverage, genuine qualifications and any verified reviews. Do not invent testimonials, promise a guaranteed result or conceal important eligibility restrictions.

7. Review the device experience and enquiry friction

On a real phone, inspect page speed, layout, cookie or popup overlays, form field count, tap targets and whether the call button works. A five-field form may be reasonable for a complex service but excessive for a simple callback request. Use analytics and actual user feedback rather than assuming the page design is the cause of poor performance.

8. Follow the enquiry through to an actual customer conversation

Check whether new enquiries arrive in the right inbox or CRM, whether someone responds during business hours and whether the response answers the customer's question. Distinguish a completed form from a qualified lead, a booked appointment and a paying customer. An account can report conversions while the business receives little commercial value. Keep personal customer details out of public reports.

Three illustrative Greater Manchester examples

Hypothetical Oldham electrician: A search campaign receives clicks for emergency work, but the business only offers scheduled installations. Before buying more traffic, inspect search terms and make the actual service and response hours clear. Do not imply a real Kydos client achieved these results.

Hypothetical Stockport beauty clinic: An ad promotes a specific consultation, but the page opens with a broad menu of unrelated treatments. Test a relevant treatment page with an easy, functioning appointment path. Follow applicable advertising rules for the treatment; avoid unsupported medical claims.

Hypothetical Manchester B2B supplier: The form works and leads arrive, but enquiries come from consumers rather than trade buyers. Review the ad wording, search terms, business eligibility and lead qualification before assuming the bid strategy alone is the problem.

Worked calculation, hypothetical only: If a campaign spends £300 and receives 100 clicks, its average cost per click is £3. If it produces two verified enquiries, cost per enquiry is £150. That is not automatically good or bad: whether the campaign is viable depends on the value of a qualified customer, conversion to a sale, gross margin and capacity. If those two events are only button clicks, the true cost per enquiry is still unknown.

When should you pause, repair or continue?

Pause or limit spend if the contact path is broken, the ad promotes a service you cannot deliver or traffic is clearly outside your intended customer base. Repair measurement first if you know genuine enquiries exist but tracking reports zero. Continue a controlled test if tracking is reliable, search terms are relevant and the business can afford to learn from a limited sample. No single click count or time period guarantees that a campaign will become profitable.

Common questions

Is it normal to get Google Ads clicks but no leads?

It can happen, particularly with small samples or competitive searches, but it is not enough information to judge the campaign. Check conversion recording, actual search terms, location targeting and the landing page before increasing spend.

Should I change to broad match to get more enquiries?

Not automatically. Broad match can reach additional searches, but it needs suitable conversion measurement, search-term review and budget control. If the problem is irrelevant traffic, expanding match types without diagnosis may make it worse.

Can I run Google Ads on £100 a week?

Possibly, for a tightly defined offer and geography, but feasibility depends on click costs, margins and how many genuine enquiries are needed. A small budget is not a guarantee of leads. Include management fees separately when comparing total cost.

Why does Google Ads show conversions when I have no customers?

A conversion action may count a softer event such as a form start, button click or unqualified enquiry. Inspect the exact action definition and compare it with completed CRM records, without exposing customer details.
