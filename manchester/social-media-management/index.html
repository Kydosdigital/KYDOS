What could the monthly output actually look like?

Three posts per week gives a business enough room to build a repeatable content rhythm without posting for the sake of volume. A sensible calendar usually mixes trust, expertise, service information, objection handling and direct conversion content.

Sample 4-week Manchester content calendar

  • Week 1: problem-led carousel, short-form video explaining the service, trust or credibility post.
  • Week 2: FAQ post, behind-the-scenes/process content, service page or enquiry CTA.
  • Week 3: common misconception, short educational video, checklist or customer decision guide.
  • Week 4: founder/team content, practical advice post, direct service reminder or offer.
  • Stories: two to three times per week to support visibility between feed posts.

The topics should change by industry. The same template should not be handed to every client.

When is social media management not enough?

Organic social media management is useful for consistency, trust and ongoing brand presence. But if the business expects a predictable flow of new leads from social media, paid acquisition may need to enter the plan.

That is a different job. Paid social requires campaign objectives, audience decisions, creative testing, media budget, landing-page alignment and conversion tracking. If a lead arrives, the business may also need CRM and follow-up processes.

What should you measure from social media management?

If the purpose is organic management, reporting should show whether the account is becoming more consistent, whether content is being consumed and whether social media is contributing to useful actions. Relevant metrics can include reach, engagement, profile actions, website visits, enquiries and assisted conversions where tracking allows.

Avoid treating follower growth as the only measure of success. A Manchester business with a smaller relevant audience can get more commercial value than a business with a large but poorly matched following.

Questions to ask before hiring a Manchester social media manager

  1. Which platforms are included? Do not assume every channel is covered.
  2. How many posts are included? Ask for a clear monthly or weekly cadence.
  3. Who creates graphics and video? This can dramatically change the true cost of the service.
  4. Is strategy included? Posting without a content plan can create activity without direction.
  5. Are stories included? Check whether they sit inside or outside the package.
  6. Who approves content? Understand the workflow before the first month starts.
  7. Is community management included? The Starter package is not the same as higher packages that add broader management.
  8. Are paid ads included? Organic management and paid advertising are separate scopes.
  9. What happens if I need leads? Ask whether the provider can support ads, landing pages, CRM and follow-up if required.
  10. What will be reported? Make sure reporting reflects the objective, not just attractive vanity metrics.
