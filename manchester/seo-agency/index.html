Manchester search strategy

Local SEO should follow your real customer footprint, not a list of place names.

A Manchester business may sell only inside the city, across Greater Manchester, throughout the North West or nationally. Those are different search problems. We start by identifying where customers can actually be served and which service-plus-location combinations deserve their own page.

That matters because creating dozens of thin local pages is not the same as having a local SEO strategy. A useful Manchester page should explain the service, local relevance, buyer objections and next step better than a generic page with “Manchester” inserted into it.

What the first 90 days should answer

A useful SEO retainer should become more specific as the data improves.

We do not believe a sensible SEO plan is a 12-month list of identical monthly tasks. The first phase should establish what the site is currently doing, where the strongest commercial opportunity sits and which technical or content constraints need to be removed first. After that, priorities should change as pages begin to gain impressions, rankings and enquiries.

01

Baseline the commercial search market

Map priority services, locations and buyer-intent keywords against the pages that already exist. This is where we decide what should be improved, what genuinely needs a new page and what should be left alone to avoid cannibalisation.

02

Remove the biggest blockers

Technical crawl/indexing issues, weak page structure, thin commercial content and poor internal linking can all suppress otherwise useful pages. The order matters: fixing the highest-impact blockers first is usually more valuable than spreading effort equally across the whole site.

03

Build authority around proven demand

Once the commercial page set is clear, supporting content, local relevance and off-page work can reinforce those targets. We would rather build a smaller coherent cluster around valuable queries than publish dozens of articles with no clear parent page.

04

Use Search Console as a feedback loop

Impressions often increase before clicks. That data shows which query variants Google is beginning to associate with a page, where titles may need improving and where two pages may be competing for the same search. The SEO plan should react to that evidence.

What we will not do

Good SEO sometimes means saying no to more pages.

Search visibility is not improved by publishing every keyword variation as a separate URL. If “SEO agency Manchester” and “SEO company Manchester” have the same commercial intent, one excellent page should normally own both. The supporting content should answer different buyer questions such as pricing, contracts, comparisons and what is included.

The same principle applies to location pages. A business serving Greater Manchester does not automatically need a landing page for every town. We validate whether the local search demand and customer journey are distinct enough to justify another page.

Why this matters

It protects authority instead of splitting it.

Clear page ownership makes internal linking, measurement and future optimisation easier. When Search Console later shows two pages repeatedly competing for the same query, we would rather consolidate them than pretend more URLs always mean more reach.

There is no responsible fixed timeline for every website. Results depend on competition, current authority, technical health, content quality, implementation speed and search-engine response. Kydos reports progress against the agreed priorities rather than promising a universal ranking date.
