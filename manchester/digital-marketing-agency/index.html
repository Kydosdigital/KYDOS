What proof should you expect before hiring a marketing agency?

What if you need leads, not just content?

This is where social media management and social media marketing become different buying decisions. A business may only need consistent content, or it may need a full acquisition system where ads drive people to a landing page, the enquiry enters a CRM, the lead is acknowledged immediately and the team can see what happened next.

If you are comparing Manchester agencies because you need leads, ask whether the agency is only responsible for the advert or whether it can also help with the landing page, tracking and follow-up. A low cost per lead means very little if enquiries go cold because nobody responds, or if the form attracts the wrong type of customer.

SEO, PPC or paid social: which service should a Manchester business buy first?

The answer should come from buying intent and urgency, not from whichever service the agency wants to sell.

  • Choose SEO first when people already search for your service and you want to build longer-term organic visibility around high-intent searches.
  • Choose PPC or Google Ads first when search demand exists and you want faster testing data or immediate visibility for selected services.
  • Choose paid social first when the offer benefits from visual explanation, retargeting, audience creation or demand generation rather than relying only on active searches.
  • Choose CRM and automation first when leads are already arriving but response, ownership or follow-up is inconsistent.
  • Choose a landing page or website improvement first when traffic exists but the page is unclear, slow, generic or difficult to convert on mobile.
A useful agency should sometimes tell you not to buy another channel yet. If your existing traffic is being wasted by a broken conversion or follow-up process, fixing that can be a better first investment than adding more traffic.

10 questions to ask before choosing a digital marketing agency in Manchester

  1. What exactly is included at the advertised starting price? Ask for deliverables, frequency, channels and exclusions.
  2. Who actually creates the work? Understand who writes copy, makes graphics, edits video and manages campaigns.
  3. What will you measure? Likes and impressions are not enough if your objective is enquiries or sales.
  4. Can you help beyond one channel? This matters if the problem turns out to be the website, landing page or CRM rather than traffic.
  5. Is ad spend included? With Kydos, paid media spend is separate from management.
  6. How will leads be followed up? Ask what happens after someone fills a form or sends an enquiry.
  7. What will the first 30 to 90 days focus on? A good answer should involve prioritisation and testing, not doing everything at once.
  8. Can you show the scope before I sign? You should know the platforms, deliverables and responsibilities.
  9. How will you adapt the strategy to my industry? Generic calendars and generic landing pages usually weaken conversion.
  10. Can I see the current pricing? Decision-stage buyers should be able to understand the starting point before a sales conversation.

Why affordability matters, but “cheap marketing” can become expensive

An affordable agency should reduce the barrier to getting professional support, not reduce the work to meaningless activity. If a £165 social media package gives you a structured calendar, designed content, video editing and regular publishing, you can compare that against the cost and time of coordinating those tasks yourself.

At the same time, buying the cheapest possible campaign can cost more when there is no tracking, the creative is weak, the landing page does not match the advert or leads are not followed up. The buying decision should be based on cost relative to useful output, not price in isolation.
