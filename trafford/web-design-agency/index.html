Trafford website architecture

One borough can contain very different customer journeys.

For a professional firm serving Altrincham and Sale, trust, expertise and a clear consultation route may carry most of the conversion weight. A home-service business serving Urmston and Stretford may need service-area clarity, rapid contact options and mobile-first forms. A Trafford Park supplier may need technical detail, sector pages and stronger qualification rather than dozens of small local pages.

The location architecture should therefore follow real intent. If a business genuinely needs different pages for different areas or services, each page should answer a different buyer question. If the answer would be identical, a stronger Trafford or Manchester parent page is usually better than a thin town swap.

Buyer decisions

Compare web design proposals by responsibility, not just appearance.

Content responsibility

Who writes, supplies and signs off the copy, imagery, case studies and legal content?

Technical responsibility

Who handles hosting, domains, DNS, email, integrations, redirects and analytics access?

Ownership

What does the client own at launch, and are there licences or subscriptions that continue?

After launch

Who fixes issues, makes changes, updates plugins or handles new requirements once the project closes?

Conversion readiness

The website should be usable before it is beautiful.

Mobile users should be able to understand the service, read long headings without clipping, use navigation, tap phone or WhatsApp actions, complete forms and view tables without document-level horizontal scrolling. Kydos treats those behaviours as release criteria rather than post-launch polish.

Mobile QA

Commercial pages are checked at 390px and 430px widths for document overflow, navigation fit, wrapping, contained tables and usable forms and contact actions.
