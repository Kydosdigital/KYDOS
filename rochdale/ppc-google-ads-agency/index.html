Rochdale geography

Rochdale's wider Greater Manchester catchment should not make the campaign geography vague.

A business may serve Rochdale town centre, Middleton, Heywood, Littleborough, Milnrow and Manchester, or only a much tighter catchment. The campaign should reflect fulfilment reality rather than simply targeting all of Greater Manchester because the platform makes it easy.

If lead quality differs by area, geography can become part of the optimisation. If it does not, splitting the account into too many tiny local campaigns can make learning slower and management more complicated.

Conversion tracking

Clicks are not the KPI if the business needs leads.

Primary conversions should represent meaningful actions such as a qualified form submission, phone call, booking or purchase. Secondary actions can help diagnose the journey, but they should not inflate lead reporting.

Account ownership

Your business should not lose its campaign history when it changes provider.

Where platform structure allows it, the business should retain appropriate ownership and administrator access to advertising accounts, conversion data and key assets. An agency may need management access, but the client should understand exactly what happens if the relationship ends.

This also applies to analytics, landing pages and CRM data. The more paid media is connected to the rest of the customer journey, the more important it becomes to document which accounts belong to the business and which tools are supplier-specific.

Before onboarding

Ask who creates the account and who owns it.

A clean answer now can prevent expensive migration problems later.

When should spend increase?

Budget should scale because the evidence is improving, not because the platform recommends it.

A larger budget can generate more data, but it can also amplify a weak offer, poor targeting or low-converting landing page. Before spend increases, the business should be able to see that conversion tracking is trustworthy, lead quality is acceptable and there is additional demand worth buying.

Tracking stable?

Primary conversions are firing correctly and not inflated by soft actions.

Lead quality acceptable?

The sales team recognises enough conversions as genuine opportunities.

More demand available?

Search impression share, keyword coverage or profitable audiences suggest additional budget can be used intelligently.

Campaign structure

Do not mix every service and audience into one campaign just because it is easier to set up.

When several services have different values, search behaviour or landing pages, they often need enough separation to control budget and measure performance. The same applies when a business serves Rochdale and Manchester differently. Structure should make the account easier to learn from, not more complicated for its own sake.

Separate by commercial logic

Services with different margins, conversion actions or intent may deserve separate budget control.

Keep data usable

Too many tiny campaigns can slow learning. Split only when the distinction changes a decision.

Match the landing page

The ad promise, keyword intent and destination page should tell the same commercial story.
