Salford website architecture

Local pages should reflect how customers actually choose you.

A Salford service business may genuinely serve Eccles, Swinton, Worsley, central Salford and customers across Manchester. The website structure should follow that real commercial footprint. One strong Salford page can often do more than a collection of thin pages for every nearby place, unless the services, customer journeys or search demand are genuinely different.

For businesses near the Manchester boundary, the important SEO question is not whether to repeat both place names everywhere. It is which page should own the core service intent, which locations need separate support and how internal links make the relationship clear.

SEO before launch

Web design and SEO should agree on the URL structure before the new site replaces the old one.

If an existing website already receives organic traffic, a redesign needs more than visual approval. Important URLs, titles, internal links and redirects should be considered before launch so a new design does not accidentally erase search equity.

Migration questionWhat should happen
Which old URLs receive useful traffic?Keep them where possible, or redirect them intentionally to the closest relevant replacement.
Are several pages being merged?Choose the strongest new destination rather than redirecting everything to the homepage.
Are new local pages being added?Check that each has a distinct purpose and does not duplicate an existing Manchester, Salford or national page.
Are forms and tracking changing?Re-test conversions after launch rather than assuming old analytics events still work.
